# Espresso

The cup is the shot. This entry describes what the shot's numbers usually have to do when nothing is added after the portafilter.

## What this type covers

The shot as it comes out, at any ratio: ristretto, normale, lungo, allongé, turbo and SOUP shots, and anything drunk straight. The ratio table and the shot styles are in Espresso Brewing Basics, which describes the shot whatever the drink, so they are not repeated here. A cup with water, milk, ice or a syrup added is another type.

## What the shot has to do in this cup

Taste right on its own. There is no milk to cover sharpness and no water to soften it, so acidity, sweetness, body and finish are all tasted directly. A fault another drink would hide is plainly there, and balance matters more here than in any other type.

## What it usually means for the shot's numbers

These are tendencies, and the rules and the Recipe's own shots outrank them.

- **Ratio** follows the character wanted, since no drink dictates it. What each ratio tends to taste like is in the ratio table of Espresso Brewing Basics.
- **Roast and body.** Medium-dark roasts on a traditional profile suit a heavy, syrupy cup. Light and light-medium roasts tend to show their best drunk straight, in a turbo or allongé style that favours clarity over body, and are judged on clarity.
- **Temperature** moves with the roast, hotter for lighter roasts and cooler for darker ones (Temperature Guidelines by Roast, in Espresso Brewing Basics).

## What tastes wrong in this cup

- **Sour, sharp, thin** usually points to under-extraction: a grind that is too coarse, a shot that ran fast or stopped early, or a temperature that is too low.
- **Bitter, harsh, dry** usually points to over-extraction: a grind that is too fine, a shot that ran long, or a temperature that is too high.
- **Sour and bitter together** usually points to uneven flow through the puck, so puck preparation comes before the grind.
- **Hollow and watery at a long ratio** often means the ratio is longer than the coffee can carry.

## How the drink notes matter

For a straight shot there is usually little to say. What belongs in the notes is how it is served: the cup, a glass of water beside it, a sugar cube. If the notes describe something poured into or over the shot, the drink is probably not Espresso and another type fits better.
