# Latte

Espresso with a lot of milk. This entry describes what the shot usually has to do when milk is most of the cup.

## What this type covers

A lot of milk, from about 1 part coffee to 4 parts milk and up: a latte, an iced latte, a mocha and drinks like them. The usual proportions:

| Drink | Espresso | Milk | Foam | Total Volume | Espresso:Milk | Vessel |
|-------|----------|------|------|-------------|---------------|--------|
| Latte | 2 oz (double) | 8-10 oz | Thin foam layer | ~10-12 oz | 1:4-5 | 8-12 oz glass or ceramic |

The smaller milk drinks (macchiato, cortado, piccolo, cappuccino, flat white) have their own entry.

## What the shot has to do in this cup

Have enough body and intensity to be tasted at all. With four or five parts of milk to one of coffee, a delicate shot disappears and the cup tastes of warm milk. Sweet, heavy, chocolatey notes tend to come through; subtle acidity and floral notes mostly do not.

## What it usually means for the shot's numbers

These are tendencies, and the rules and the Recipe's own shots outrank them. The shot's character tends to point to a format:

| Shot Character | Recommended Format | Milk Volume | Why |
|----------------|-------------------|-------------|-----|
| Intense, heavy body, bold | Latte (if desired) | 8-10 oz | More milk balances intensity |

- **Ratio.** Many cafés pull about 1:2 for milk, as the traditional style does (Espresso Brewing Basics). With this much milk the shorter end, 1:1 to 1:1.5, tends to help: more intensity per millilitre of milk, which is what the rule for milk drinks and ristretto says.
- **Roast and body.** Medium to dark roasts with heavy body and bold, bittersweet flavour tend to hold up. A very light, clarity-focused roast tends to lose its character here, and a smaller format may serve it better.
- **Temperature** follows the roast (Temperature Guidelines by Roast, in Espresso Brewing Basics). A dark roast at the cooler end of its range avoids harshness that milk does not cover.

## What tastes wrong in this cup

- **Tastes only of milk** means the shot is too delicate for the volume: a shorter ratio, a larger dose or a darker roast adds intensity, and a smaller format is the other way out.
- **Bitter and harsh through the milk** usually points to over-extraction or a roast that is too dark: a coarser grind or a cooler shot.
- **A sour, sharp edge under the milk** usually points to under-extraction: a finer grind or a slightly hotter shot.
- **Thin, watery** often means the shot is too long for the milk.
- **Scalded, flat, bitter** that no change to the shot's numbers fixes is usually milk heated past 70°C.

## How the drink notes matter

With this much milk, the milk is half of what is in the cup. Whole dairy gives the best foam and medium sweetness, barista-edition oat is the best alternative and tends to taste sweeter, soy is acid-sensitive, and almond and coconut foam poorly. Syrups and chocolate add sweetness and body that cover subtle flavours, so a flavoured latte needs less delicacy and more intensity from the shot. For an iced latte, ice dilutes as it melts, so a more concentrated shot tends to be needed than for the same drink hot. Foam style and milk temperature are in Milk & Drink Preparation.
