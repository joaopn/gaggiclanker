# Cappuccino

Espresso with a little milk. This entry describes what the shot usually has to do when a small amount of milk is added and the coffee is still meant to be tasted.

## What this type covers

A little milk, up to about 1 part coffee to 3 parts milk. The drinks that belong here:

| Drink | Espresso | Milk | Foam | Total Volume | Espresso:Milk | Vessel |
|-------|----------|------|------|-------------|---------------|--------|
| Macchiato | 1 oz (single) | 0.5 oz | Dollop of foam | ~2 oz | 1:0.5 | 2-3 oz demitasse |
| Cortado | 2 oz (double) | 2 oz | Thin microfoam | ~4 oz | 1:1 | 4 oz glass (Gibraltar) |
| Piccolo | 1 oz (single ristretto) | 2-3 oz | Thin microfoam | ~3.5 oz | 1:2.5 | 3-4 oz glass |
| Cappuccino (traditional) | 2 oz (double) | 2 oz | 2 oz stiff foam | ~6 oz | 1:1:1 | 5-6 oz ceramic |
| Cappuccino (modern) | 2 oz (double) | 4 oz | Microfoam throughout | ~6 oz | 1:2 | 6 oz ceramic |
| Flat white | 2 oz (double) | 4 oz | Very thin microfoam | ~6 oz | 1:2 | 5-6 oz ceramic |

Extract for the bean's best expression, then match the right drink format. The latte, with a lot more milk, has its own entry.

## What the shot has to do in this cup

Keep its character through the milk. Acidity and fruit should still show, and the milk should lift the sweetness instead of burying it.

## What it usually means for the shot's numbers

These are tendencies, and the rules and the Recipe's own shots outrank them. The shot's character tends to point to a format:

| Shot Character | Recommended Format | Milk Volume | Why |
|----------------|-------------------|-------------|-----|
| Bright, fruity, delicate | Cortado or piccolo | 2-3 oz | Small milk volume preserves acidity and fruit |
| Sweet, balanced, medium body | Cappuccino or flat white | 4-6 oz | Enough milk to complement without drowning |
| Clarity-focused (turbo/allongé) | Cortado or piccolo | 2-3 oz | Lighter body gets lost in large drinks |

- **Ratio.** Many cafés pull about 1:2 for milk, as the traditional style does (Espresso Brewing Basics). A shorter shot, 1:1 to 1:1.5, gives more intensity per millilitre of milk, which is what the rule for milk drinks and ristretto says.
- **Roast and body.** Sweet, balanced, medium-bodied shots from medium roasts are the easy case. Bright light roasts keep their fruit best in the small formats.
- **Temperature** follows the roast (Temperature Guidelines by Roast, in Espresso Brewing Basics): a degree or two hotter if the cup is sour, cooler if it is bitter.

## What tastes wrong in this cup

- **Coffee flavour lost** means too much milk for the shot: move to a smaller format, or make the shot more concentrated.
- **Sour, sharp** usually points to under-extraction, which a little milk softens only slightly: a finer grind or a slightly hotter shot.
- **Bitter, burnt** usually points to over-extraction, or to milk heated past 70°C.
- **Thin, watery** often means the shot is too long.

## How the drink notes matter

The milk changes the cup as much as the shot does. Whole dairy is the standard. Barista-edition oat is the best alternative for foam and tends to taste sweeter, soy is acid-sensitive and can curdle with bright shots, and almond and coconut foam poorly. A syrup adds sweetness that covers acidity, so a bright shot tastes less bright. State the milk, its amount and the foam style. Steaming and milk temperature are in Milk & Drink Preparation.
