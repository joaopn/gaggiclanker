THE RULES THAT MATCH (planned style: bloom — the profile to fork (Medium 18g 1:2))
dial_in_order:
  [grind_before_profile] If grind is plausibly the problem, propose no profile change at all. A profile edit made while the grind is wrong hides the grind error and costs two shots to unpick.
  [hierarchy] Work the variables in order and stop at the first that explains the problem: 1 grind (shot time more than 5 s off target, or clearly sour or bitter), 2 dose and yield (time is fine but the taste is not — sour or salty means +5 g out, bitter or drying means -5 g out), 3 temperature (flat, sharp, or no sweetness), 4 pressure and profile (character rather than extraction), 5 puck prep (sour AND bitter together, or shot-to-shot inconsistency).
  [one_variable_at_a_time] Change one variable at a time. Two changes in one shot make the next cup uninterpretable, and the whole point of a Set version is to be able to say what a single change did.
  [taste_is_ground_truth] The user's tasting notes outrank the telemetry. Sour, weak or fast means under-extracted; bitter, harsh or slow means over-extracted — whatever the curve looked like. Never contradict a recorded taste with a number.

increments:
  [grind] Move the grinder in its own smallest meaningful step — one or two — and say which way in the grinder's units, never in microns.
  [preinfusion_flow] Adjust pre-infusion flow in 0.5 ml/s steps.
  [preinfusion_time] Adjust pre-infusion in 2-3 s steps.
  [pressure] Adjust pressure in 0.5-1 bar steps.
  [temperature] Adjust brew temperature in 1 °C steps; 2 °C at most in one move.
  [yield] Adjust yield in 5 g steps — about half a ratio point on a normal dose.

temperature_by_roast:
  [light] Light roasts extract at 94-96 °C: the cell structure is dense and needs the heat.

pressure_matrix:
  [compression_floor] A puck compresses at about 4 bar, so pre-infusion belongs below that: pressure applied before the bed has set is what opens a channel.
  [decline_compensates_erosion] A declining pressure phase compensates for the puck eroding: as resistance falls, dropping the setpoint keeps flow where it was instead of letting the shot run away at the end.
  [flow_peak] Flow through an espresso puck peaks around 9 bar. Above about 10 the extra pressure goes into deforming the bed rather than through it.
  [washed.light] Washed light: 8-9 bar. A clean, dense bean takes full pressure.

ratio_by_style:
  [bloom] A bloom profile aims at about 1:2 to 1:2.5; the soak does the work, not the ratio.
  [ristretto_and_milk] For milk drinks and ristretto, 1:1 to 1:1.5 is the working range.

time_by_style:
  [bloom] A bloom profile totals 30-45 s, with first drip between 15 and 25 s.

profile_design_defaults:
  [bloom] A bloom is the pump OFF for 5-15 s — not the valve closed. The valve stays at 1.
  [hold] Hold for 15-30 s with a volumetric target on it, so the shot ends on weight and not on time.

equipment_hints:
  [conical] Conical burrs make more fines: run 7-8 bar with a gentle ramp and a longer pre-infusion, and expect a tighter bed at the same setting than a flat burr would give.
  [distribution_before_tamping] Distribution matters more than tamping, and tamping more than pressure. Almost every inconsistency that survives a grind change is a distribution problem.
  [dose_is_the_basket] Dose to the basket's rating plus or minus 1 g. Underdosing leaves headspace, water pools above the puck and channels; a deeper basket wants a coarser grind for the same time.

safety_bounds:
  [never_invent_data] Never state a measurement that is not in the context. If the dose, the grind setting or the taste is missing, ask for it in questions_for_user rather than assuming a value.
  [one_step_at_a_time] Never suggest more than one step of one variable in a single shot, and never a change larger than the increment rules allow. A big correction is two shots, not one.
  [pressure] Never suggest a pressure outside 0-12 bar; the firmware itself refuses more.
  [temperature] Never suggest a brew temperature outside 25-100 °C.
