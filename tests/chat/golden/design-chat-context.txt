# Cappuccino

Espresso with a little milk. This entry describes what the shot usually has to do when a small amount of milk is added and the coffee is still meant to be tasted.

## What this type covers

A little milk, up to about 1 part coffee to 3 parts milk. The drinks that belong here:

| Drink | Espresso | Milk | Foam | Total Volume | Espresso:Milk | Vessel |
|-------|----------|------|------|-------------|---------------|--------|
| Macchiato | 1 oz (single) | 0.5 oz | Dollop of foam | ~2 oz | 1:0.5 | 2-3 oz demitasse |
| Cortado | 2 oz (double) | 2 oz | Thin microfoam | ~4 oz | 1:1 | 4 oz glass (Gibraltar) |
| Piccolo | 1 oz (single ristretto) | 2-3 oz | Thin microfoam | ~3.5 oz | 1:2.5 | 3-4 oz glass |
| Cappuccino (traditional) | 2 oz (double) | 2 oz | 2 oz stiff foam | ~6 oz | 1:1:1 | 5-6 oz ceramic |
| Cappuccino (modern) | 2 oz (double) | 4 oz | Microfoam throughout | ~6 oz | 1:2 | 6 oz ceramic |
| Flat white | 2 oz (double) | 4 oz | Very thin microfoam | ~6 oz | 1:2 | 5-6 oz ceramic |

Extract for the bean's best expression, then match the right drink format. The latte, with a lot more milk, has its own entry.

## What the shot has to do in this cup

Keep its character through the milk. Acidity and fruit should still show, and the milk should lift the sweetness instead of burying it.

## What it usually means for the shot's numbers

These are tendencies, and the rules and the Recipe's own shots outrank them. The shot's character tends to point to a format:

| Shot Character | Recommended Format | Milk Volume | Why |
|----------------|-------------------|-------------|-----|
| Bright, fruity, delicate | Cortado or piccolo | 2-3 oz | Small milk volume preserves acidity and fruit |
| Sweet, balanced, medium body | Cappuccino or flat white | 4-6 oz | Enough milk to complement without drowning |
| Clarity-focused (turbo/allongé) | Cortado or piccolo | 2-3 oz | Lighter body gets lost in large drinks |

- **Ratio.** Many cafés pull about 1:2 for milk, as the traditional style does (Espresso Brewing Basics). A shorter shot, 1:1 to 1:1.5, gives more intensity per millilitre of milk, which is what the rule for milk drinks and ristretto says.
- **Roast and body.** Sweet, balanced, medium-bodied shots from medium roasts are the easy case. Bright light roasts keep their fruit best in the small formats.
- **Temperature** follows the roast (Temperature Guidelines by Roast, in Espresso Brewing Basics): a degree or two hotter if the cup is sour, cooler if it is bitter.

## What tastes wrong in this cup

- **Coffee flavour lost** means too much milk for the shot: move to a smaller format, or make the shot more concentrated.
- **Sour, sharp** usually points to under-extraction, which a little milk softens only slightly: a finer grind or a slightly hotter shot.
- **Bitter, burnt** usually points to over-extraction, or to milk heated past 70°C.
- **Thin, watery** often means the shot is too long.

## How the drink notes matter

The milk changes the cup as much as the shot does. Whole dairy is the standard. Barista-edition oat is the best alternative for foam and tends to taste sweeter, soy is acid-sensitive and can curdle with bright shots, and almond and coconut foam poorly. A syrup adds sweetness that covers acidity, so a bright shot tastes less bright. State the milk, its amount and the foam style. Steaming and milk temperature are in Milk & Drink Preparation.

THE RULES THAT MATCH (planned style: bloom — the profile to fork (Library 18g))
dial_in_order:
  [grind_before_profile] If grind is plausibly the problem, propose no profile change at all. A profile edit made while the grind is wrong hides the grind error and costs two shots to unpick.
  [hierarchy] Work the variables in order and stop at the first that explains the problem: 1 grind (shot time more than 5 s off target, or clearly sour or bitter), 2 dose and yield (time is fine but the taste is not — sour or salty means +5 g out, bitter or drying means -5 g out), 3 temperature (flat, sharp, or no sweetness), 4 pressure and profile (character rather than extraction), 5 puck prep (sour AND bitter together, or shot-to-shot inconsistency).
  [one_variable_at_a_time] Change one variable at a time. Two changes in one shot make the next cup uninterpretable, and the whole point of a Recipe version is to be able to say what a single change did.
  [taste_is_ground_truth] The user's tasting notes outrank the telemetry. Sour, weak or fast means under-extracted; bitter, harsh or slow means over-extracted — whatever the curve looked like. Never contradict a recorded taste with a number.

increments:
  [grind] Move the grinder in its own smallest meaningful step — one or two — and say which way in the grinder's units, never in microns.
  [preinfusion_flow] Adjust pre-infusion flow in 0.5 ml/s steps.
  [preinfusion_time] Adjust pre-infusion in 2-3 s steps.
  [pressure] Adjust pressure in 0.5-1 bar steps.
  [temperature] Adjust brew temperature in 1 °C steps; 2 °C at most in one move.
  [yield] Adjust yield in 5 g steps — about half a ratio point on a normal dose.

temperature_by_roast:
  [light] Light roasts extract at 94-96 °C: the cell structure is dense and needs the heat.

pressure_matrix:
  [compression_floor] A puck compresses at about 4 bar, so pre-infusion belongs below that: pressure applied before the bed has set is what opens a channel.
  [decline_holds_flow] A declining pressure phase compensates for the puck wearing down: as resistance falls, dropping the setpoint keeps flow where it was instead of letting the shot run away at the end.
  [flow_peak] Flow through an espresso puck peaks around 9 bar. Above about 10 the extra pressure goes into deforming the bed rather than through it.
  [washed.light] Washed light: 8-9 bar. A clean, dense bean takes full pressure.

ratio_by_style:
  [bloom] A bloom profile aims at about 1:2 to 1:2.5; the soak does the work, not the ratio.
  [ristretto_and_milk] For milk drinks and ristretto, 1:1 to 1:1.5 is the working range.

time_by_style:
  [bloom] A bloom profile totals 30-45 s, with first drip between 15 and 25 s.

profile_design_defaults:
  [bloom] A bloom is the pump OFF for 5-15 s — not the valve closed. The valve stays at 1.
  [hold] Hold for 15-30 s with a volumetric target on it, so the shot ends on weight and not on time.

equipment_hints:
  [conical] Conical burrs make more fines: run 7-8 bar with a gentle ramp and a longer pre-infusion, and expect a tighter bed at the same setting than a flat burr would give.
  [distribution_before_tamping] Distribution matters more than tamping, and tamping more than pressure. Almost every inconsistency that survives a grind change is a distribution problem.
  [dose_is_the_basket] Dose to the basket's rating plus or minus 1 g. Underdosing leaves headspace, water pools above the puck and channels; a deeper basket wants a coarser grind for the same time.

safety_bounds:
  [never_invent_data] Never state a measurement that is not in the context. If the dose, the grind setting or the taste is missing, ask for it in questions_for_user rather than assuming a value.
  [one_step_at_a_time] Never suggest more than one step of one variable in a single shot, and never a change larger than the increment rules allow. A big correction is two shots, not one.
  [pressure] Never suggest a pressure outside 0-12 bar; the firmware itself refuses more.
  [temperature] Never suggest a brew temperature outside 25-100 °C.
