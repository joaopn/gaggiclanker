[ESPRESSO_TASTING_GUIDE#what-your-taste-tells-you] What Your Taste Tells You
## What Your Taste Tells You

For full diagnostic decision trees with branching logic and style-relative thresholds, see `gaggimate://knowledge/diagnostics/DIAGNOSTIC_TREES.md`.

| You taste... | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Sour + thin | Under-extraction |
| Sour + fast shot | Grind too coarse |
| Salty or savory | Severe under-extraction |
| Bitter + slow shot | Over-extraction |
| Bitter + dry finish | Severe over-extraction |
| Flat/lifeless | Stale beans or wrong temp |
| Good but lacks body | Ratio too long or channeling |
| Harsh/ashy | Over-extracted or dark roast issue |
| **Sour AND bitter** | **Channeling** — uneven extraction |

**The "sour AND bitter" rule (Scott Rao):** When a shot tastes both sour and bitter simultaneously, water is finding paths of least resistance — over-extracting some grounds while under-extracting others. Primary fix: improve puck prep (WDT, distribution, even tamp). Do NOT grind finer — that increases puck resistance and worsens channeling. Grinding slightly *coarser* may help by reducing resistance, but only after addressing distribution. Also consider: extend pre-infusion, reduce pre-infusion flow, or add a bloom phase.

## Common Flavor Descriptors

Use these if you want to be more specific:

**Positive:**
- Fruity (berry, citrus, stone fruit)
- Sweet (caramel, honey, chocolate)
- Nutty (almond, hazelnut)
- Floral (jasmine, rose)

**Negative (defects):**
- Sour, acidic, sharp (under-extracted)
- Bitter, burnt, ashy (over-extracted)
- Dry, astringent (over-extracted)
- Flat, lifeless (stale or wrong temp)
- Watery, thin (under-extracted)

## Notes for Different Shot Styles

The balance expectations differ by style:

| Style | Expected Character | "Off" would be... |
|---|---|---|
| **Traditional** (9 bar, 25-35s) | Full body, balanced, syrupy | Thin, watery, or harsh |
| **Turbo** (6 bar, 10-20s) | Sweeter, less body, clarity | Muddy or bitter |
| **Allongé** (longer ratio) | Lighter body, bright | Watery without complexity |
| **SOUP** (<2 bar) | Tea-like, juicy, clear | Thin without flavor |

Don't judge a turbo shot for lacking body — that's expected. Judge it on clarity and sweetness.

## Interpretation Cheat Sheet

### Grind Too Fine

- `max_pressure_overshoot_bar` > 1.5 (MODERATE/SIGNIFICANT_OVERSHOOT)
- Resistance avg HIGH or VERY_HIGH
- Flow avg below expected for style
- Channeling risk may be elevated

### Grind Too Coarse

- Pressure never reaches target (`max_pressure_undershoot_bar` high)
- Resistance avg LOW or VERY_LOW
- Flow avg above expected for style
- Fast extraction time

### Channeling

- `channeling_risk` HIGH or VERY_HIGH (ignore INSUFFICIENT_DATA — it means the phase was too short to assess)
- Resistance slope STEEP_DECLINE
- Pressure volatility JITTERY or VOLATILE
- Flow acceleration in late shot

### Temperature Problems

- `stability_std_c` > 1.5 (UNSTABLE) → equipment issue
- `overshoot_c` > 2.0 → thermosiphon or PID issue
- `undershoot_c` > 2.0 → machine not heated properly

### Good Shot Indicators

- Resistance MODERATE, STABLE, GRADUAL_DECLINE
- Channeling LOW
- Temperature VERY_STABLE or STABLE
- Profile adherence EXCELLENT or GOOD
- Overshoot WITHIN_TOLERANCE

[COFFEE_PROCESSING#the-methods/natural-dry-process] The Methods > Natural (Dry Process)
### Natural (Dry Process)

**What happens:** Whole cherries are dried intact on raised beds or patios for 2-4 weeks. The seed ferments inside the fruit the entire time, absorbing sugars, esters, and organic acids from the decomposing mucilage and skin.

**Flavor signature:** Heavy body, intense sweetness, fruit-forward (blueberry, strawberry, tropical fruit). Can have winey or fermented notes. Acidity is lower and rounder than washed. At its best: juicy and complex. At its worst: boozy, overripe, or "dirty."

**Why it extracts the way it does:** Extended fruit contact produces beans with:
- Higher sugar content (caramelizes easily — risk of bitterness at high temps)
- More volatile fermentation compounds (extract very quickly)
- Lower density and more irregular internal structure (less uniform extraction)

This means naturals extract faster and less evenly than washed coffees. Over-extraction of fermentation compounds creates harsh, boozy, or muddy flavors.

**Espresso implications:**
- **Lower pressure** (1-2 bar below what you'd use for a washed coffee of the same roast) — reduces extraction of harsh fermentation byproducts
- **Bloom profiles recommended** — even saturation compensates for uneven bean structure
- **Extend pre-infusion** — gentle wetting reduces channeling risk from porous pucks
- **Temperature: lower end for roast level** — limits caramelization of high sugar content
- **Puck prep is critical** — uneven density means channeling is more likely

**Common origins:** Ethiopia (Guji, Sidamo natural lots), Brazil (common for commodity and specialty), Yemen

dial_in_order:
  [grind_before_profile] If grind is plausibly the problem, propose no profile change at all. A profile edit made while the grind is wrong hides the grind error and costs two shots to unpick.
  [hierarchy] Work the variables in order and stop at the first that explains the problem: 1 grind (shot time more than 5 s off target, or clearly sour or bitter), 2 dose and yield (time is fine but the taste is not — sour or salty means +5 g out, bitter or drying means -5 g out), 3 temperature (flat, sharp, or no sweetness), 4 pressure and profile (character rather than extraction), 5 puck prep (sour AND bitter together, or shot-to-shot inconsistency).
  [one_variable_at_a_time] Change one variable at a time. Two changes in one shot make the next cup uninterpretable, and the whole point of a Set version is to be able to say what a single change did.
  [taste_is_ground_truth] The user's tasting notes outrank the telemetry. Sour, weak or fast means under-extracted; bitter, harsh or slow means over-extracted — whatever the curve looked like. Never contradict a recorded taste with a number.

increments:
  [grind] Move the grinder in its own smallest meaningful step — one or two — and say which way in the grinder's units, never in microns.
  [preinfusion_flow] Adjust pre-infusion flow in 0.5 ml/s steps.
  [preinfusion_time] Adjust pre-infusion in 2-3 s steps.
  [pressure] Adjust pressure in 0.5-1 bar steps.
  [temperature] Adjust brew temperature in 1 °C steps; 2 °C at most in one move.
  [yield] Adjust yield in 5 g steps — about half a ratio point on a normal dose.

temperature_by_roast:
  [high_altitude] Beans grown above about 1 800 m are denser: add 1-2 °C and grind a step finer than the roast level alone would say.
  [light] Light roasts extract at 94-96 °C: the cell structure is dense and needs the heat.
  [processing_offset] More fermentation means more soluble material: heavily fermented lots (natural, honey, anaerobic) extract a degree or two cooler than a washed bean of the same roast, with a longer pre-infusion and a coarser grind.
  [taste_adjustment] Within the roast's window, sour moves the temperature up 1-2 °C and bitter or astringent moves it down 1-2 °C — after grind and yield have been ruled out, not before.

pressure_matrix:
  [compression_floor] A puck compresses at about 4 bar, so pre-infusion belongs below that: pressure applied before the bed has set is what opens a channel.
  [decline_compensates_erosion] A declining pressure phase compensates for the puck eroding: as resistance falls, dropping the setpoint keeps flow where it was instead of letting the shot run away at the end.
  [flow_peak] Flow through an espresso puck peaks around 9 bar. Above about 10 the extra pressure goes into deforming the bed rather than through it.
  [natural.light] Natural light: 7-8 bar — more soluble than washed, so less pressure.

ratio_by_style:
  [bloom] A bloom profile aims at about 1:2 to 1:2.5; the soak does the work, not the ratio.
  [ristretto_and_milk] For milk drinks and ristretto, 1:1 to 1:1.5 is the working range.

time_by_style:
  [bloom] A bloom profile totals 30-45 s, with first drip between 15 and 25 s.

band_meanings:
  [channeling_risk:LOW] channeling_risk LOW — no indicator crossed its threshold; the puck held.
  [flow_adherence:GOOD] flow_adherence GOOD — RMSE 0.3-0.8 ml/s against the commanded flow.
  [flow_overshoot:MINOR_DEVIATION] flow_overshoot MINOR_DEVIATION — a small, measurable departure above target flow.
  [pressure_adherence:EXCELLENT] pressure_adherence EXCELLENT — RMSE under 0.3 bar; the machine tracked the profile closely.
  [pressure_overshoot:WITHIN_TOLERANCE] pressure_overshoot WITHIN_TOLERANCE — under 0.25 bar over target; deviation means nothing here.
  [resistance_erosion:GRADUAL_DECLINE] resistance_erosion GRADUAL_DECLINE — a slow fall, normal as the bed saturates.
  [resistance_level:MODERATE] resistance_level MODERATE — 1.5-3.0, the usual range for a well-packed basket.
  [temperature_stability:STABLE] temperature_stability STABLE — std 0.3-0.8 °C, within normal variation.

taste_to_suspect:
  [sour] Sour: under-extracted. Expect high flow, an early first drip, or a low temperature. Grind finer first; if it is already fine, add 5 g of yield and a degree of heat.
  [watery] Watery or thin: low pressure and high flow. That is a strength problem, not an extraction one — shorten the ratio or raise the dose before changing the grind.

profile_design_defaults:
  [bloom] A bloom is the pump OFF for 5-15 s — not the valve closed. The valve stays at 1.
  [hold] Hold for 15-30 s with a volumetric target on it, so the shot ends on weight and not on time.

equipment_hints:
  [conical] Conical burrs make more fines: run 7-8 bar with a gentle ramp and a longer pre-infusion, and expect a tighter bed at the same setting than a flat burr would give.
  [distribution_before_tamping] Distribution matters more than tamping, and tamping more than pressure. Almost every inconsistency that survives a grind change is a distribution problem.
  [dose_is_the_basket] Dose to the basket's rating plus or minus 1 g. Underdosing leaves headspace, water pools above the puck and channels; a deeper basket wants a coarser grind for the same time.

safety_bounds:
  [never_invent_data] Never state a measurement that is not in the context. If the dose, the grind setting or the taste is missing, ask for it in questions_for_user rather than assuming a value.
  [one_step_at_a_time] Never suggest more than one step of one variable in a single shot, and never a change larger than the increment rules allow. A big correction is two shots, not one.
  [pressure] Never suggest a pressure outside 0-12 bar; the firmware itself refuses more.
  [temperature] Never suggest a brew temperature outside 25-100 °C.
